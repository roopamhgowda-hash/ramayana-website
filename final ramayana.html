about ramayana

The Ramayana is an ancient Sanskrit epic poem from India that narrates the life and moral journey of Rama, the prince of Ayodhya and the seventh avatar of the Hindu deity Vishnu

Key Facts

• Author: Traditionally attributed to the sage Valmiki.
• Composition Time: Written or transmitted around the middle centuries of the 1st millennium BCE (approx. 500 BCE to 100 BCE).
• Structure: Comprises nearly 24,000 couplets (shlokas) divided into seven chapters called kandas (Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kishkindha, Sundara, Yuddha, and Uttara).
• Core Theme: A timeless exploration of dharma (duty, righteousness, honor, and moral obligation) triumphing over adharma (evil).

The Story Overview

• Exile: Prince Rama is wrongfully sent into a 14-year forest exile due to a promise his father, King Dasharatha, made to Queen Kaikeyi. His loyal wife, Sita, and brother, Lakshmana, accompany him.
• Abduction: The demon king Ravana of Lanka abducts Sita during their exile.
• The War: Rama forms an alliance with an army of forest-dwellers and monkey warriors led by Sugriva and Hanuman. They march to Lanka, defeat Ravana, and rescue Sita.
• Return & Rule: Rama returns to Ayodhya to claim his rightful throne—an event celebrated during Diwali.

Cultural Impact

• Festivals: Celebrated widely through festivals like Dussehra (the defeat of Ravana) and Diwali (Rama's homecoming).
• Global Influence: Adapted into various regional texts (such as Tulsidas's Ramacharitramanas) and traditional performance arts across Southeast Asia, including Thailand, Indonesia, and Cambodia.
