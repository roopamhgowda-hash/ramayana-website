About Ramayana

The Ramayana is an ancient Sanskrit epic poem from India that narrates the life and moral journey of Rama, the prince of Ayodhya and the seventh avatar of the Hindu deity Vishnu.

Key Facts

  • Author: Traditionally attributed to the sage Valmiki.
  • Composition time: Middle centuries of the 1st millennium BCE (approx. 500–100 BCE).
  • Structure: Nearly 24,000 couplets (shlokas) divided into seven kandas (Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kishkindha, Sundara, Yuddha, Uttara).
  • Core theme: Dharma (duty, righteousness) triumphing over adharma (evil).

Story Overview

  • Exile: Prince Rama is sent into a 14-year exile; Sita and Lakshmana accompany him.
  • Abduction: Ravana abducts Sita during exile.
  • The War: Rama allies with Sugriva and Hanuman and defeats Ravana to rescue Sita.
  • Return & Rule: Rama returns to Ayodhya — celebrated during Diwali.

Cultural Impact

Festivals like Dussehra and Diwali, adaptations such as Tulsidas's Ramacharitramanas, and performance traditions across Southeast Asia show the Ramayana's wide influence.
